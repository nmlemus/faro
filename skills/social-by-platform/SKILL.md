# Content by platform

The cross-channel part — territories, calendar, measurement — is in `social` and
`content-strategy`. This covers only **what changes depending on where you publish**.

> ⚠️ Anything algorithm-dependent ages fast. Treat it as a starting point, not as verified
> fact. What does **not** age is the format and audience part.

## LinkedIn — B2B, authority, lead generation

- **The first hours decide.** Publishing and not answering comments wastes the post.
- **The founder's personal brand outperforms the company page.** If the client must choose
  where to invest, it is there.
- Personal story + business lesson is the combination that travels furthest.
- **Native content beats external links** — pulling people off-platform is penalised. If you
  must link, put it in the first comment.
- Formats: short post with a hook, long story, article, document carousel.
- Typical deliverables: post, story, article, profile optimisation, network strategy.

## Twitter / X — speed, reach, conversation

- The hook is the first line. Without a hook there is no second line.
- Threads develop an idea; single posts get reach.
- Publishing often beats publishing perfectly.
- Replying to large accounts with something substantive gets more reach than posting alone.

## Instagram — visual, aspirational, discovery

- The first image or the first 3 seconds decide whether they scroll on.
- Carousel to teach, reel for reach, story for relationship.
- The caption serves the image, not the other way round.
- Saves and shares matter more than likes.

## TikTok — attention, native, rhythm

- The first 2 seconds are everything. No exceptions.
- Native beats produced. A polished agency video is spotted and skipped.
- Sound matters; silence kills reach.
- Trend + your own angle beats a copied trend.

## YouTube — search intent, depth, retention

- It is a search engine, not a social network. Title and thumbnail do 80% of the work.
- Retention rules: the first 30 seconds decide the reach of the whole video.
- Good for content people deliberately search for ("how to X"), not for interruption.
- A good video pays out for years; a social post pays out for hours.

## Reddit — community, zero selling

- **Read the community before posting.** Every subreddit has its own rules and enforces them.
- Self-promotion is punished fast and sometimes permanently.
- Contribute before asking, with history. A new account posting its product is spam.
- Better for audience research than for distribution.

## Pinterest — visual discovery, long life

- It is a visual search engine: pins keep bringing traffic months later.
- Vertical, legible text over the image, and the destination must deliver what it promises.
- Strong for purchase decisions on visual products; weak for B2B.

## Discord — community, retention, not acquisition

- Good for retaining and activating, not for acquiring.
- An empty community is worse than no community. Do not open the channel without someone to
  hold it.
- The host sets the rhythm: without constant presence it dies in weeks.

## Rule common to all of them

What makes an asset work is **the angle and the proof**, not the format. Adapting format to
network is the last step, not the first. And no factual claim ships without backing here
either — see house rule 1.
