# Landing page

A landing page has **one job**: get one person to do **one thing**. Anything that does not
push toward that thing is working against it.

## Before you look at the page

Answer three things. If you cannot, the page cannot be judged yet:

1. **Who arrives here and from where?** Someone who clicked an ad already knows something;
   someone from a search knows nothing. The same page does not serve both.
2. **What do they have to do?** One action, not three.
3. **What do they know and believe when they arrive?** Their level of awareness decides how
   much you must explain before you ask.

## The audit, in order

This order is not aesthetic: it is the order in which people leave.

### 1. The first five seconds
- What is this and who is it for? It has to come from the H1 and subheading, without scrolling.
- Is the outcome clear, or only the feature? "Scheduling without conflicts" beats "integrated
  calendar system".
- **Hard test:** if you paste a competitor's logo onto this copy and nobody notices, the page
  says nothing.

### 2. The action
- How many CTAs? If more than one real action competes, both lose.
- Does the button say what will happen? "Start free" > "Submit".
- Is it available when intent appears — near the top and again after the proof?

### 3. The objection
List the three reasons this person would **not** click. Find where the page answers them. If
it does not, that is the finding — not the button colour.

The objections almost always missing: what it costs, how much work it is to start, what
happens if it doesn't work, and why believe you.

### 4. The proof
- Logos without a case, testimonials without a name and numbers without a source **are not
  proof**.
- One concrete case with a name and a figure beats ten logos.
- If no proof is available, the page is written differently — not inflated. See house rule 1.

### 5. The friction
- Every form field that is not essential costs conversions.
- Is something asked before anything is given?
- Is the price there? Hiding it filters less than it costs in abandonment, except in complex
  high-ticket sales — and there it is replaced by a range, not by silence.

### 6. What happens if they don't buy today
Most traffic is not ready. If the page offers nothing to someone still deciding, that traffic
is lost entirely. Is there something to take away? Is there a reason to come back?

## Severities

- **P0** — unclear what it is, no possible action, or a claim without backing.
- **P1** — understandable and actionable, but a central objection goes unanswered.
- **P2** — incremental improvement.

## When recommending

Every change carries **what changes, why, and how it is verified**. "Improve the CTA" is not a
recommendation. "Change 'Submit' to 'See pricing' because objection #1 is cost, measured by
CTA clicks over page visits" is.

And always say what **cannot** be known without conversion data. A landing audit without
analytics judges the page's logic, not its performance.
