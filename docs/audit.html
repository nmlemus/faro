Two dead buttons on the page that recruits paying customers

This is verbatim from an audit of a marketplace app's site. It took about twenty minutes, unattended. Nobody told it to check the HTML.

05-website-audit.md — §2.3
Two of the site's core buttons are dead, and the copy tells people to click a button that doesn't exist

On the provider page — the page meant to recruit the paying side of the marketplace — the primary "Get Started" button goes nowhere:

<a class="elementor-button elementor-button-link elementor-size-sm" href="#">Get Started</a>

The App Store badge next to the working Google Play badge is also dead (href="#"), so an iPhone-using provider who scrolls to the clearest, most concrete part of the page hits a dead end. And the closing paragraph instructs the reader:

"Click 'Sign Up Your Business' and start your journey to success."

No button anywhere on the page carries that label. These are one-line fixes, but until they're made, a real share of the providers who make it furthest into the page — the ones who are actually convinced — cannot act on it.

It tells the client what it could not know

Every audit opens by drawing its own boundary. This is section 1 of the same deliverable, unedited:

05-website-audit.md — §1
What we looked at, and what we didn't

We had no access to analytics, conversion data, or revenue. That means this audit says nothing about:

· How many people visit the site, or where they come from · Where visitors actually drop off before installing · Click-through rate on any button (including the two we found broken) · Search rankings, keyword volume, or organic traffic · Cost of acquisition, or which channel produces installs

Everywhere below that a number like this would normally go, we say plainly that we don't have it — we don't guess at it.

A declared hole is worth something. A plausible invented number is what loses the account.

That paragraph is not politeness. It is the rule the system runs on: no number enters a deliverable unless it came out of something run in that session. Not from memory, not from an industry average, not from a reasonable estimate. And the last section of every audit lists exactly which access would answer each open question — which is how the next piece of work gets sold.
